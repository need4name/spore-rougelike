# From Mind to Empire

How intelligence works at the end of the Creature stage, and how it leads into the three stages after it: **Tribe**, **Society** and **Empire**.

This is the plan for Updates 13–19. **Update 13 (the Mind tree, Paths of Mind and the ten endings) is built;** the rest is not yet.

---

## The big idea

One thread runs through the whole game, from the first cell to the last empire:

**Your start → how you lived → your Path of Mind → your ending → the people you become → the empire you build.**

Two rules make the thread work:

1. **Each stage zooms out one level.**
   - Cell and Creature: you are a *lineage*, a species among species.
   - Tribe: you are a *band*, a few dozen individuals with a leader.
   - Society: you are a *people*, several settlements with rulers and neighbours.
   - Empire: you are a *realm*, a dynasty ruling much of the world.
2. **Everything you were becomes something in the next stage.**
   - Your body becomes your people's talents.
   - The other species become the animals you tame, revere or fear, and sometimes rival peoples.
   - Your ending becomes your founding myth.

So a deep-sea predator and a land grazer don't just look different. They think differently, build different societies and end up with different empires.

---

## The thread: what each stage passes on

| What carries over | Creature → Tribe | Tribe → Society | Society → Empire |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Identity** (never changes) | Path of Mind, land or sea, temperament (from your starting cell) | Same | Same |
| **The ending you reached** | Decides your kind of tribe, its first discoveries and one special rule | Decides your kind of society: settled, roaming or warlike | Decides your kind of empire: conquest, trade, faith or wonder |
| **Your body** | Parts become talents: hands craft, voices sing, glow lures, venom poisons, shells armor, wings scout | Talents become your people's strengths in traditions | Strengths shape your technology and armies |
| **Other species** | Become animals in your territory: hunt them, tame them, revere them as totems | Tamed species become herds, mounts or partners; some species become rival peoples | Rival peoples become rival empires |
| **Your history** | Your Chronicle becomes stories your leader tells | Your ending and ancestors become myths and the start of a faith | Myths become a religion that can spread and split |
| **Rivalries** | Your nemesis species becomes your band's ancestral enemy, and old allies stay friendly | Feuds and friendships carry over: an old enemy band becomes a rival people with a grudge | Old grudges become rival empires, wars of revenge and ancient alliances |

## The three stages at a glance

| | **Tribe** | **Society** | **Empire** |
|---|---|---|---|
| You control | A band of 5–30 members | 3–8 settlements | A realm of many provinces |
| The map | Your home biome up close: camps, hunting grounds, sacred sites | A region with neighbours | The whole world map |
| Instead of DNA and parts | **Ideas** and **Discoveries** (drafted and combined, like parts) | **Influence** and **Traditions and Laws** | **Prestige** and **Technologies and Edicts** |
| Characters | A **leader** with traits; a new one takes over when they die | A **ruler** and a council of three, who have opinions | A **dynasty**: ruler, heirs, spouse, rivals, vassals, schemes |
| Rivals | Wild species and, later, one rival band | Neighbour peoples of any kind | Rival empires, including ones descended from your past lineages |
| It ends when | You found a home, take to the road, or conquer | You unite your region by conquest, trade, faith or a wonder | One of your path's grand endings, which unlocks New Game+ |

Each stage keeps what already works: the real-time map, events with odds, Activities, milestones and the Chronicle. The **Mind tree** idea carries on at each stage: Discoveries in the Tribe, Traditions in Society and Technologies in the Empire, each built from roots, a trunk, branches and capstones.

## Five kinds of people

Your Path of Mind decides what kind of people you become. Each kind has a land form and a sea form, built to the same depth.

| Path | Tribe stage | Society stage | Empire stage | Special resource | What makes it play differently |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Toolmakers** | Tribe (land) / Shell Clan (sea: otters, crabs) | Hearthlands: villages and towns | Kingdom (land) / Forge-cities at the vents (sea) | **Fire** (land) / **Vent-heat** (sea) | You craft things and give them to your members. Fire and the forge lead to metal on land and electricity at sea. |
| **Singers** | Choir (land: howlers, songbirds) / Pod (sea: whales, dolphins) | Song-circles: a league along migration routes | Song-nation | **Song** | Knowledge lives in songs. If your songkeepers die before teaching, discoveries are lost. You travel huge distances and are the best at diplomacy. |
| **Many Minds** | Den (soft climbers on land, octopuses at sea) | Den Network: settlements that move and hide | Shifting Cities | **Light** (color and glow) | You have few people, but each one is brilliant. You have no fixed capital, learn by watching others (copying their discoveries), and use camouflage and disguise. |
| **The Swarm** | Hive (land mound) / Reef-colony (sea) | Hive-cities | Hive Dominion | **Brood** | You assign castes instead of individuals. There is a queen instead of a leader, and your one great building keeps growing. |
| **Gardeners** | Grove (fungus farms) / Kelp Garden (sea) | Garden League | Living Empire | **Growth** | You farm species from your Creature world and grow your technology instead of building it. Blight is your great danger. |

## Temperament: what your starting cell adds

Your starting archetype is your people's temperament. At every stage it adds one unique idea to the tree and one special rule. That is why a Hunter Toolmaker and a Herd Toolmaker feel different even though both build a Tribe.

| Start | Temperament | In the Tribe | In Society | In the Empire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Predator | **The Hunter** | Great hunts and raids; other bands fear you | Warrior lodges; you can win diplomacy through fear | Conquest is cheaper; rebellions are bloodier |
| Grazer | **The Herd** | Numbers and defense; hard to wipe out | Assemblies: the council has more say | Strong defensive leagues |
| Symbiote | **The Bond** | Your partner species joins your band: a two-species tribe | Alliances are easy | A federation of peoples |
| Parasite | **The Taker** | You live off neighbours: tribute, theft, stealing their discoveries | Tributaries | Vassal-heavy realms |
| Colony | **The Many** | Early castes and big numbers | Huge populations | Megaprojects |
| Mimic | **The Mask** | Spies and deception | Intrigue at court | Schemes: the most Crusader Kings 3 way to play |
| Drifter | **The Wanderer** | You move camp and scout | Trade routes | Explorers and colonies |

|---|---|---|

| First discoveries | Lure-lights, Venom-darts, Pressure-maps | Fire, Spears, Baskets |

The same game engine runs both. But the trees, resources, rules, myths and story all come from what each lineage did.

## How each stage plays

### Tribe: a band in its home

- **The map:** your home biome up close, with camps, hunting grounds and sacred sites. The species from your Creature world still roam it, now as wildlife.
- **Your people:** 5–30 members, drawn as a small herd. A few have names: the **leader** and the **keepers** (crafters, songkeepers, queens, gardeners, depending on your kind). The leader has traits, and when they die, someone else takes over, sometimes badly.
- **Resources:** Members, Food, Ideas and your kind's special resource.
- **Discoveries replace mutations:**
  - You choose 1 of 3, and pairs combine into something better, just as parts evolve. For example, Stick + Stone gives a Spear, and Song + Map gives a Song-map.
  - Known combinations are saved in the Codex for later runs.
- **Your body decides your talents.** Your parts make some discoveries easy:
  - hands for crafting;
  - voices for songs;
  - glow for lures;
  - venom for poison;
  - shells for armor;
  - wings for scouting;
  - many eyes for lookouts.
- **Activities:** Hunt, Gather, Tame (domesticate a species), Revere (make a species your totem), Raid, Move camp, Ceremony, Meet strangers.
- **Milestones:**
  - your first special resource: First Fire, First Song, First Light, First Queen or First Harvest;
  - your second leader;
  - strangers: the first rival band;
  - the Founding, which is the finale.
- **Three endings for each kind:**
  - **Settle** leads to a settled society.
  - **Roam** leads to a roaming society.
  - **Conquer** leads to a warlike society.

### Society: a people among neighbours

- **The map:** a region with 3–8 settlements: villages, reefs, mounds, groves or lairs.
- **Your rulers:** a ruler and a council of three. Each councillor has an opinion of the ruler, gives advice, and can work against them.
- **Traditions and Laws:**
  - This is your culture tree. It contains choices that exclude each other, such as warlike or peaceful, open or closed, faith or reason.
  - Its first node is your founding myth, taken from your Creature ending.
- **Neighbour peoples:** any kind can sit next to any other: a Tribe beside a Hive, a Pod beside a Den. Some are rival species from your Creature world that also learned to think.
- **Diplomacy:** gifts, trade, joining families, alliances, raids and wars.
- **Faith is born:** myths are built from your own Chronicle: your ancestor, your ending, the nemesis species that hunted you.
- **Four ways to end it** (plus one for each kind): unite the region by **Conquest**, **Trade**, **Faith** or a **Wonder**. The way you win decides your empire's type.

### Empire: a realm across the world

- **The map:** the World Map from Update 11 at full scale, divided into provinces, with land and sea realms.
- **A dynasty**, the heart of Crusader Kings 3: your ruler, their heirs, spouse and rivals, inheritance laws, vassals, and a council that schemes.
- **Technology:**
  - On land: fire, then metal, then engines.
  - At sea: vents, then electricity, then living light, then coral-shaping.
  - Your kind changes it again: Gardeners grow their technology and the Swarm builds it.
- **Rival empires** of other kinds, including empires descended from lineages in your Fossil Record.
- **Wars, treaties and betrayals; faiths that spread and split; wonders and disasters.**
- **Grand endings for each kind,** plus a few dead ends. Each grand ending unlocks **New Game+**: a new world that starts with echoes of your old ones.

## Runs and progression

- **Every stage ending is a checkpoint.** It earns Genetic Memory and leaves a fossil, so dying in the Tribe stage still moves you forward.
- **Stage Select:**
  - Once you have reached a stage, a new run can start there with a fossil from that point.
  - The ending your fossil reached decides what you can start as.
  - A full run from cell to empire is long, so this matters.
- **One currency, more trees.** Genetic Memory stays the only currency. The Evolution screen gains a new tab for each stage you reach:
  - **Ancestral Wisdom** for the Tribe;
  - **Heritage** for Society;
  - **Legacy** for the Empire.
- **The Codex of Endings:**
  - A grid of your 7 starts against every ending.
  - Each new square you fill unlocks a temperament flavour, a starting option or a Gene Affinity.
  - Seeing every route needs different starts and different endings.
- **Difficulty:** your first attempt at a new stage should usually fail, and upgrades should get you through, just as the Cell and Creature stages work now.

## Keeping it buildable

- **One engine per stage; kinds are data.** Tribe, Society and Empire each get one engine. The five kinds, their land and sea forms and the seven temperaments are lists of ideas, events and rules, not separate games.
- **Trees are assembled from pieces.** Every stage tree is made of:
  - roots carried from the stage before;
  - a trunk for land or sea;
  - branches for your kind;
  - capstones that are the endings.

  Any one run sees about a third of each stage's content.
- **Content budget for each stage**, about 110 events:
  - about 40 shared events, with words that adapt to your kind ("your band / pod / hive");
  - 8 signature events for each kind (40);
  - about 15 each for land and sea.
- **Two kinds first, then the rest.** Each stage ships first with one land kind and one sea kind, then the other kinds in that stage's second update.
- **Fairness is measured.** The audit tool and the test bots check every kind for land–sea balance and similar win rates.

## Decisions made

- **Path of Mind:** you choose it at the Spark of Mind, from the 2–3 Paths your body and history allow.
- **Paths are fixed for the whole run.** They never mix, even in the Empire stage, so each run stays true to its path and replaying with a different start or path is the way to see more. Conquering or allying with another kind gives you their people as subjects or friends, never their path. The Bond temperament's partner species joins your band, but you still think along your own path.
- **Societies:** one shared engine, with a resource, a culture tree and 1–2 unique rules for each kind.
- **Rival species can learn to think.** This happens rarely in the Tribe stage (a rival band appears) and often in Society. Rivalries are the backbone of the later stages: your nemesis, your allies and your feuds carry forward from stage to stage (see "Rivalries" in the table above).
- **How deep characters are at each stage:** a leader in the Tribe, a ruler and council in Society, and the full dynasty only in the Empire. **This is provisional:** it needs playtesting, and may change once you can play the Tribe stage.
- **Build order:** the Creature-stage Mind tree comes first (Update 13), then the Tribe stage with Toolmakers on land and Singers at sea.
