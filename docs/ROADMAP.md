# Primordia: roadmap

The game grows in **themed updates**, like Minecraft's. Each update has one theme, a short list of what's in it, and a test checklist. After each one you play it, send feedback, and any fixes go into a small patch (6.1, 6.2…) before the next update starts.

**Done so far:** Updates 1–5 built the Cell and Creature stages: event cards, real-time map, drafts, merging, evolutions, Mind tree, flight, symmetry and body plans.

## Ground rules

- **One theme per update.** If an idea doesn't fit the theme, it goes on the list for a later update.
- **Each update is playable on its own.** Nothing is left half-built between updates.
- **Polish comes last.** Balance tweaks, art and sound get a full pass at the end (Update 15). Between updates we only fix what blocks testing or is clearly broken.
- **Saves.** Unlocks and the Codex always carry over. A run that's in progress may restart when a new stage arrives; the game will say so.
- **What's new.** From Update 6 on, the title screen shows a short "What's new" note, so you know what to look for.

## Update 6: The Living World

*The world remembers you.*

- **Event chains:** stories that unfold over several events. For example, a wounded predator returns stronger, or a friendly species asks for help and repays it later.
- **Nemesis and ally species:** a species you've hurt or helped gets a name and a portrait, and keeps showing up in events about you.
- **Species that evolve:** other species gain parts, change size and move territory during the run, so the map changes over time.
- **Seasons and weather on the map:** winters, droughts and floods you can see, each with matching events and food changes.
- **Migrations:** herds cross the map, newcomers arrive and leave, and some events only happen while they're passing.
- **What's new screen** on the title page.

**You'll test:** whether stories feel connected rather than random, and whether the map feels alive.

## Update 7: Wild Places

*Different worlds play differently.*

- **Biomes:** each run's world is made of regions such as tundra, desert, swamp, jungle, reef, kelp forest and abyss. Each region has its own scenery, food, events and part drafts.
- **Moving regions:** migrate your herd to a new region partway through the run, with risks along the way.
- **From sea to land (and back):** sea creatures can crawl onto land, and land creatures can return to the water, as whales did. It costs you something, and it opens new parts.
- **Biome parts:** for example, fat layers for cold, water storage for desert, pressure skin for the abyss, and gills plus lungs for the shoreline.
- **New home worlds** to unlock, built around the biomes.

**You'll test:** whether different regions make you build different creatures.

## Update 8: Bloodlines

*Every run leaves a mark.*

- **Challenge runs:** modifiers you can turn on for a bigger reward. For example: no carnivores, only one draft per milestone, or permanent winter.
- **Achievements**, each with an unlock (for example, "win with a serpent" unlocks the Serpent archetype).
- **Fossil record:** a gallery of every lineage you've played, with its portrait, how it ended and what it unlocked.
- **Past lineages in the world:** creatures from your earlier runs sometimes appear as rival species.
- **Daily run:** the same world and seed for everyone each day.
- **More archetypes and boons** to unlock.

**You'll test:** whether you want to play "just one more run", and whether unlocks feel worth chasing.

## Update 9: First Fires (Tribe stage, part 1)

*Your creatures become a people.*

- **Leaving the Creature stage:** the Creature finale now leads into the Tribe stage. Your ending (Firekeepers, Wanderers and so on) sets your starting bonus.
- **The village on the map:** your huts, fires and gatherers. Your old Creature-stage species are now animals you can hunt, herd or befriend.
- **Tribe resources:** Population, Food and DNA become Tribespeople, Food and **Ideas**.
- **Discoveries replace body-part drafts:** spears, baskets, fire-hardened tools, songs, burial rites. Some discoveries combine into better ones, the way parts evolve.
- **Chief:** your tribe has a named chief with traits. When they die, another takes over.
- **About 25 Tribe events** and a first Tribe finale.
- **Stage select:** once you've reached a stage, a new run can start there with a lineage you've already played, so you don't have to replay everything.

**You'll test:** whether the Tribe stage feels different from the Creature stage while still feeling like the same game.

## Update 10: Neighbours (Tribe stage, part 2)

*You are not the only people.*

- **Rival tribes** on the map, each with its own chief, culture and opinion of you.
- **Diplomacy:** trade, gifts, marriages between chiefs, raids and alliances.
- **Culture tree:** the Mind tree grows into myths, crafts and customs. Choices exclude each other, for example warlike or peaceful.
- **Totems and rituals:** big one-off choices that shape the rest of the run.
- **Three Tribe endings**, each leading into the Civilization stage: unite the tribes by friendship, by force, or by trade.

**You'll test:** whether dealing with other tribes is the interesting part, and whether the endings feel earned.

## Update 11: Crowns (Civilization stage, part 1)

*The most Crusader Kings 3 part of the game.*

- **A regional map** of cities you found and grow.
- **A ruler and a dynasty:** your ruler has traits, a spouse, heirs and rivals. Succession can go wrong.
- **A council:** advisors who give advice, scheme and sometimes betray you.
- **Three ways to grow,** as in Spore: military, trade or faith.
- **Technologies** take over from drafts, as discoveries did in the Tribe stage.
- **About 30 Civilization events,** many built around characters.

**You'll test:** whether your ruler and their family feel like people you care about.

## Update 12: Empires (Civilization stage, part 2)

*The whole world, one way or another.*

- **Rival nations** with rulers of their own, plus wars, treaties and betrayals.
- **Faiths** that spread, split and clash.
- **Wonders:** big projects that take a long time to build and give large rewards.
- **Disasters:** plague, famine and rebellion.
- **Civilization endings** that lead into the Space stage, plus a few "dead end" endings that still earn Genetic Memory.

**You'll test:** whether the late game stays tense rather than turning into a victory lap.

## Update 13: Lift-off (Space stage, part 1)

*Leave home.*

- **Your ship,** shown on a star map of nearby systems.
- **Exploring:** planets to scan, settle or terraform. Planets come from the same biomes as Update 7.
- **Space events:** strange signals, derelict ships, living planets.
- **Colonies** that grow on their own and sometimes send events back to you.

**You'll test:** whether space feels like a new frontier rather than the same game with a new background.

## Update 14: The Galaxy (Space stage, part 2)

*Everyone you've ever been is out there.*

- **Aliens from your past runs:** lineages from your fossil record show up as alien empires, with the bodies you built for them.
- **Galactic diplomacy:** alliances, wars and trade between empires.
- **The true ending:** reach the galactic core. Beating it unlocks **New Game+**, where a new world starts with echoes of your old ones.

**You'll test:** whether meeting your old creatures is a fun surprise, and whether the ending feels big enough.

## Update 15: The Final Polish

*Everything we've been saving for the end.*

- **Balance pass** across every stage, using the test bots and your notes.
- **Art pass:** better drawings, animations and scenes.
- **Sound and music:** ambient sounds per stage, plus sounds for choices, evolutions and scenes.
- **Tutorial:** a gentle first run that teaches the game as you play it.
- **Offline app:** installs to your home screen and works without internet.
- **Settings:** text size, reduced motion and colour-blind palettes.
- **Performance** on older phones.

**You'll test:** the whole game, from start to finish, with fresh eyes.
